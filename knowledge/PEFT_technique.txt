**PEFT** stands for **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning**. It is a technique used in machine learning to adapt large, pre-trained AI models for specific tasks without needing to retrain or modify all of the model's parameters.

### Why is PEFT needed?

Large AI models contain billions of parameters. Traditional fine-tuning requires updating every single parameter, which demands massive computing power, expensive GPUs, and large amounts of storage space for every new task.

### How PEFT Works

Instead of changing the entire model, PEFT **freezes the core pre-trained model** so its original knowledge remains untouched, and it only trains a very small fraction of extra parameters or layers (often less than 1% of the total model).

### Popular PEFT Techniques

* **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** The most popular method. It adds small, trainable matrices into the model layers to handle new tasks while keeping the original model weights frozen.
* **Adapters:** Inserting small custom neural network layers into the model architecture for a specific job.
* **Prompt Tuning:** Adding trainable virtual tokens to the input text to guide the model's output without altering its internal weights.

### Key Benefits

* **Lower Costs & Hardware Requirements:** You can fine-tune huge models using much less memory and cheaper hardware.
* **Prevents Forgetting:** Because the main model weights stay frozen, the AI does not lose its general knowledge when learning a new task.
* **Tiny File Sizes:** Instead of saving a massive multi-gigabyte copy of the model for every project, you only save tiny adapter files (sometimes just a few megabytes) that attach to a single base model.
